My tips? Well...

Prebuff. This is so important, it bears repeating. Prebuff! Pro-Fire and
Pro-Magic-Energy for everybody, Spell Immunity : Abjuration & Divination
(in conjunction with Improved Invisibility), Stoneskin and Mirror Image for
your Mages, and Hardiness for your Fighters. Don't bother with prebuffs
with duration shorter than a turn.

Dispel 'em. This should be your first priority during the battle. The
Eclipse party have buffs flying up the wazoo, and damaging them without
dispelling them is nearly impossible. Don't be afraid to stack x3 Remove
Magic in a Sequencer to increase the odds.

Pro-Magic-Weapons. As you already noticed, the fighters hit fast, and hit
hard. The Anti Paladin is also capable of dispelling your Stoneskin.
However, they are not smart enough to switch to mundane weapons when
necessary -- so Pro-Magic-Weapons will grant you 4 rounds of safety. Trust
me -- it'll save your skin during the Total Eclipses. Obvisously, this is
only to be cast once the battle starts.

Don't bother to remove their spell protections. Bombard them with
Area-of-Effect spells, instead, which will cut right through their
protections. Do, however, remove their combat protections, so your fighters
can do what they do best.

They ignore summons. This can be either a bane or a boon. Summoning
Skeleton Warriors, Mordies or any sort of "distracting" summons is useless.
Summoning a Planetar is an *excellent* idea, though. 

Pile on! Don't bother with one-on-one magic or melee duels. You will lose
them in the next Total Eclipse. Instead, use your mages to occupy the
fighters while your fighter pile on the spellcasters.

Do not waste Wilting and the like on the shades. A simple Turning will do.

Do use high level spells liberally. Improved Alacrity is mandatory if you
wanna be as fast as they are, Wilting is an excellent way to cut through
spell protections and Dragon's Breath is especially useful. The knockback
effect will help you isolate your targets.

Speed is your friend here. Use Improved Haste on all your fighters.

Sola's special ability is ideal for this sort of battles. Aeire's under
attack? Solaufein for the rescue!

Teleport Field is a wonderful way to neutralize dangerous fighters. By
messing up their "personal initiative rounds", it prevents them from
attacking at all if stacked enough -- because unlike most disablers, it
does not allow a save! Kensai, where's thy sting? 

Nothing unique, here - it's all about finding their weaknesses.


Here's what Gwion has to say about the Eclipse 
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

I /studied/ these guys. I did a number of test runs - reloading once I'd
seen how they reacted to a particular situation or variation. My party had
- me (FMT) Jaheira, Minsc, Imoen, Sola & Sarevok. When I thought that I
knew enough I tried more seriously and only had Jaheira die - some nasty
moments with the others aye, but strategic invisibility saved lives.

Primary advice - there's so much happening at once here that's its crashed
my computer a few times! In order to cut down the amount happening at once
(and to help you keep up with events) turn autopause on. I recommend you
tag _every_ choice possible. There's a lot going on at once, you don't want
to miss any of it.

*Strengths and Weaknesses.*

Weakness: They only appear when Solaufein and the main protagonist are
outdoors, at night and fairly near each other. Consequently, traps and
summons can be set up /before/ these circumstances are met...

Weakness: They concentrate on the character with the worst AC. OK, this is
hideous, but it can be exploited if you're careful. e.g.  With me this is
usually Imoen, who happens to carry the Staff of Magi. (and why not?) With
boots of speed and popping into and out of visibility, she can lead some of
them quite a merry dance.

Weakness: They ignore invisible characters. How to exploit - if someone's
in real trouble, try to turn invisible. If you do it in time, they'll leave
that one alone.

Weakness: They split into 2 groups. The fighters advance while the casters
stay back. It's possible to draw the fighters out of sight of the casters
if there are some summons waiting at the entry point.  These tie up the
casters and don't let them be as effective as they could be.

Weakness: The cutscene doesn't pause the game (obviously - but it's still a
weakness) - any spells initiated before it starts will continue to be cast.
The game doesn't pause until the talking starts. Doesn't sound much? Hah!
This can be exploited - see below!

Weakness: given a choice, the Eclipse ignore summoned creatures entirely.
That's fine against the lesser summons, but I wouldn't be inclined to
ignore a Planetar or Elemental Prince if I could help it
- Those guys are /powerful/ and the Planetar can cast a lot of useful
  spells. Insect Plague anyone?

Strength: Using Shadowkeeper, I couldn't find any spells memorised by the
casters - this means that the scripts must be casting the spells, which
renders the casters immune to Hakeashar and Nishruu (was this deliberate or
a side effect?)

Strength: The Eclipse seem immune to regular and spike traps. Must be that
immunity to missiles bit. They're *highly* resistant to fire (I think you
need to strip them before they take ANY fire damage) so the exploding trap
is wasted, and they love time traps - you're in a worse position than they
are! The Bounty Hunter Spcial Snare is also unlikely to be of much use,
although I wouldn't object to a /really/ high level trap mazing someone -
it'd take some of the pressure off.  Only the sorceror has a good Int, so
if you *do* maze someone, they'll be out for a while. Good luck. On the
whole, traps are of limited use against the Eclipse themselves. However:

Strength: JUST before they appear, some Devil Shades appear. These take out
any traps and can really hurt you if left unattended. This means that the
Eclipse won't trigger traps when they appear. Devil shades are immune to
fire, so don't bother with the exploding traps.  Just use the regular traps
and set some spikes a long way from the point the Eclipse appear. That way,
the same Devil Shade won't trigger them all. Remember, a Devil Shade will
spawn 2 Shadow Fiends when they die. Not as bad, but still quite a
nuisance.

Strength: The Eclipse are far more effective with their high level
abilities than you can ever be. Why? Because each one is permanant!
Waiting around for the effects to wear off (which I've used to great effect
myself at other times) doesn't work. If you manage to evade them for an
hour - their abilities are still running.

Weakness: They have no more high level abilities available. Once you've
dispelled their buffs, they're gone. I've yet to see the fighters re-buff.
The casters will of course, but we try to ignore those don't we.

Strength: The group can time stop, and any one of them can trigger it. Yep,
the only guy left, who is from a class renowned for not casting spells, can
time stop. Sola is immune. This is good because otherwise you're in real
trouble. Even so, if you're using the decoy tactic of the Staff of Magi
you'd better be invisible when this goes off. Otherwise they'll catch up
and shred that character.

Weakness: The Eclipse always appear in the same positions relative to the
party. Once you know it, you can exploit it. 2 devil shades appear next to
the main protagonist. (might I suggest having Anomen or similar standing
next to this one and be already turning undead?) 1 devil shade appears next
to Solaufein. Radael appears by Solaufein, and the other five are slightly
further back from him.  The casters to the rear, the fighters to the fore.
Sensible, but exploitable.


*Personal tactics.* Know when they're coming. Expect them to come. Ensure
that when they come, it's on YOUR terms. Sure you have to spend the night
hours indoors, but this gives you the chance to work on your sequencers and
contingencies. Keep Sola indoors while the others go outside to set traps
(I know that there's fodder sent early, but if you don't kill it, it'll
hurt you. This is NASTY fodder) and summon critters.  Recommendations: A
Mordy's Sword or 2, a Planetar, an Elemental Prince etc. Get the really
powerful stuff out (trust me, not Hakeashar - no appreciable effect on
them) and get all 5. Set them up by the doorway that Sola will come out of.
Set most of the traps there too. Set some other traps a distance away - 3/4
of the way to vision's edge. This is where your folks will be based.
Remember that the critters may have short summon durations, so don't waste
time.  Set up the combat buffs before you summon them. Next, select Sola
from indoors, send him out and then unpause. He'll step through and there's
a good second or two before the Eclipse appear by him. The moment he's
stepped through, pause and send him to join the party.  Get everyone who
can to cast discriminatory area spells (any spell which can affects several
enemies but doesn't touch allies - Wilting, Dragon's Breath etc.) at the
summons, *especially* Creeping Death and Insect Plague. The Eclipse appears
where he was when they were triggered. Now the fun thing is, you're casting
some nasty spells before the targets are available, but they'll be there
before the spell is finished. Understand? This is the perfect use for
Creeping Death and I've had a single use of this cause major damage to
those casters. Two are better - they may resist one and the quicker they
die the better. The casters are unable to use spells and take horrific
damage for the duration.

Unfortunately, the 3 fighters are scary. They're tough, fast, well-prepared
and well-equipped. Draw them away from the casters as soon as possible and
make certain that you concentrate on the Anti-Paladin. His sword can dispel
magic on contact, just like Carsomyr. Since you've essentially put the
magic users out of commision, or quite likely killed some/all outright
(just takes 1 insect swarm - planetar - 1 creeping doom - Jaheira - and
some luck with the other stuff) that's the guy who will be hurting your
buffs.  A word of warning, mirror image and stoneskin are of /very/ minor
use - they go too quickly. They're better than nothing, but protection from
magic weapons is far more useful - provided you can keep it up. Concentrate
on a single target rather than splitting onto lots of different ones. Why?
As they can regenerate, you need to cause the maximum damage to one of them
in the minimum amount of time. The combination of their potions and
regeneration means that they'll get healed quickly. Take down the
Anti-Paladin first, the Kensai second and the Barbarian last. That's the
order of priority.  Unless you've got a great AI available, turn it off.
You can't afford to have one of them decide to do something that he
shouldn't be doing. Hopefully the fighters will have been infected with
some of those nasty bugs and be taking damage. *Every* little helps here,
so wait until you're a distance away from the casters before you cast
remove magic on the fighters (*Not* dispel - you do want to keep your own
buffs up don't you) Now that you've done this, they're a *lot* more
vulnerable - not as strong, not as resistant etc.

Good Luck - These guys are tough.


Here's what Rassadihn has to say about the Eclipse 
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
You defeated Undead Sola. You danced around the Revenge Spiders. You even
killed Archryssa in one-on-one combat. You're on top of the world. Then, the
Eclipse comes and kills you - after delivering some scorching desert dry
meta-humor à la Ilyich.

Now, as odd as this may seem: they're not that tough. You're just not using
your own potential properly. By the time you hit 4M XP average, your party
should be able to breeze through just about anything in the game, even
something with as big an advantage as the Eclipse fellows get. It took me
four very frustrating tries to defeat them (having your entire party killed
in under a minute disheartens anyone), but each attempt fared better than
the last, so I didn't despair.

Undead Sola is all about getting through impossible defenses. Improved Bodhi
is all about surviving impossible attacks. The Eclipse is all about knowing
yourself. You actually stand a good chance against anything if you just do
what the Eclipse does, in the opposite direction. Their temporal antics
should not be a match for your wits.

I don't think it needs a blow-by-blow walkthrough. It's fairly
straightforward if you both prepare before the battle and know what to do
during. If you fail to do either, you're dead. Simple as that.

So, what should you prepare with? Aside from the standard buffs (Stoneskins,
Fireshields et al.), consider the following:

- Spell Immunity: Abjuration and Divination on every mage who can cast it.
The Eclipse will try hard to dispel your buffs in general and invisibility
in particular, but they don't cheat by being immune to it (how refreshing).
If one of your spellcasters gets in serious trouble, invisibility is then an
option. Undead Sola taught me this neat combo. :-)

- Pro Magic Energy on as many people as possible. I gave Tashia this one a
lifetime ago, and I've never regretted it. That they keep you safe from
enemy Wiltings is good, but that they keep you safe from friendly Skull
Traps is even better. Bombs away!

- Hardiness on all your fighters. Unlike most other high-level abilities,
Hardiness actually lasts a while, so you *can* use it before the battle.

- Resist Fire/Cold and Protection from Fire. These (divine) spells are so
cheap that there's really no excuse for not getting everyone to >100%
resistance before the battle. Then laugh at the sorcerer when he casts
Dragon's Breath. But not too hard - you may still be knocked back. :-)

- *No* Pro Magic Weapons or anything else that lasts less than, say, ten
rounds. By the time you're facing them, they're gone. You can't afford to
waste anything here.

During the battle, you may want to consider these do's and don'ts:

- DO use Improved Alacrity, but DO NOT use Time Stop (for obvious reasons).

- DO put a Remove Magic in a Contingency or Sequencer somewhere. Stripping
the buffs from the fighters should be your top priority, but not if it keeps
you from casting Pro Magic Weapons right off the bat.

- DO use Pro Magic Weapons liberally. They'll keep you safe during those
Total Eclipses. DO NOT let them run out on you, under any circumstances.
Even if they're buffed to the max, your casters can still be killed easily,
especially if their buffs are being dispelled by the Anti-Paladin.

- DO use Spellstrike, Ruby Ray, Khelben's Warding Whip and Breach to wear
down the spellcasters' defenses. DO NOT use Pierce Magic or Pierce Shield;
they're not dragons. It's tempting to sweep all the protection dispellers on
one big, generic heap, but resist this temptation and use the right spell
for the right job. Exception: spell restrictions on Sequencers may force you
to use a lower-level spell.

- DO use powerful summons that can hit back, like Planetars and Elemental
Princes. DO NOT use distracting summons like Mordies or Skeleton Warriors.
The fighters won't feel them and the spellcasters know how to get rid of
them. (I peeked into their script to make sure; personally I didn't even try
it - this *is* a Weimer battle, after all.)

- DO use your invulnerable mages (right?) to distract the enemy fighters,
your fighters to kill one spellcaster at a time and your summons to do
whatever the situation demands (usually, distracting another spellcaster).
DO NOT get bogged down in one-on-one melee or a magical duel; such tactics
will not survive a Total Eclipse.

- DO use Dragon's Breath yourself, preferably after a Greater Malison.
Knocking people back is a wonderful way of isolating them.

- DO use Sola's Dimension Door to interpose attacks on defenseless people.
He does what he must, when he must. Know this well. ;-)

- DO Turn Undead whenever your cleric doesn't have a useful spell to cast.
DO NOT take the Devil Shades for granted, they'll make you regret it. If you
have Daystar, DO use it.

- DO NOT panic... I mean, DON'T PANIC!


Gratuitous extra things I learned from this battle, not part of the actual
tactic:

Suppose a Greater Whirlwinding Sarevok attacks something with the Ravager
+6. If it doesn't have Pro Magic Weapons or Death Ward on it, it's dead.

Devas blow. Planetars rule. Which is a bit odd - it's not like mages are
short on summoning spells, or the cleric repertoire couldn't use a boost.

Once sorcerers can cast Improved Alacrity, anybody else short of another
sorcerer can pack up and go home, because they don't stand a chance of
defeating them. During none of my attempts was Tashia ever in any danger
while nuking things from orbit.

In general, a mage-heavy party will be at a disadvantage when you start the
game, but by the end they'll be so powerful that it isn't funny anymore.

Lack of Pro Magic Weapons makes druids, even multiclassed ones, very
vulnerable. Even clerics have Sanctuary, but druids are stuck with Ironskins
and Shield of the Archons. Nice, but not enough. If you're planning on
casting spells, don't even think about it if there isn't a wall of fighters
between you and the enemy.

R.
